Game Description:

A Galaga clone. The player controls a ship that moves left and right with A/D or the 
arrow keys, and fires with space. Coins are inserted with 5 and the game is started with 1.

Each level spawns 5 waves of 8 enemies that fly in along Bezier curves and move into
a formation before diving at the player. The player starts with 3 lives. Clearing every
enemy advances to the next level.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Feature Implementation:

Engine:
- Multi-shape collision, where objects break down into component shapes and directed
  lines report their first point of contact.
- Two-phase collision detection, bounding boxes first and component shapes only on
  overlap.
- Object transforms, so objects draw and collide in their own local space.
- A separate draw context for the GUI.
- Cubic Bezier curves over multiple segments, giving position and slope along a path.
- Notification manager, holding registrations through weak pointers.
- Keyboard events routed into the notification manager, on key down and key up.
- Object iteration over the engine's objects.

Galaga:
- Galaga class running the game state machine, wave spawning, score, lives and GUI.
- Enemy entry along Bezier paths into formation, with deterministic attack runs.
- Three enemy types with their own artwork, paths and firing patterns.
- Bullets on the new collision API, terminating at the point of contact.
- Player and Enemy1 built from multiple collision components.

Design Decisions/Changes to basic API:

- Collision bounds and shapes are in local coordinates around the object's origin
  rather than world space. The engine applies each object's location and rotation
  before testing them.
- A destroyed bullet draws its impact circle growing from radius 2 to radius 12 over
  four frames before it is removed, rather than a single fixed circle.
- Held movement keys are tracked with a flag set on key down and cleared on key up, and
  the flag is applied once per frame in Update to make movement smoother.
- Difficulty rises with the level number. Attack runs begin at one attacker every 36
  frames and tighten to four attackers every 12 frames by level 13.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Galaga Class Game State Flow:

1. Waiting.
Shows INSERT COIN, or PRESS 1 TO START once a coin is in. 5 adds a coin, 1 resets score,
lives and level, then moves to Startup.

2. Startup.
Spawns the player ship and shows the level number for two seconds, then moves to Waves.

3. Waves.
Spawns 8 enemies per wave for 5 waves, each with its entry path. Once all 40 have reached
formation, moves to Playing.

4. Playing.
Enemies attack and the player fights. Clearing the level returns to Startup, a death with
lives left moves to Respawn, and losing the last life moves to GameOver.

5. Respawn.
Waits for attacking enemies to return to formation, shows the remaining lives, then
spawns the player and returns to Playing.

6. GameOver.
Shows GAME OVER for four seconds, clears the objects, and returns to Waiting.

Player deaths during enemy entry are handled after all ships have completed their entry
paths.

Attackers are chosen on a fixed frame interval, taking the enemy in formation closest
to the player's x position, with ties broken by position.

Extra Credit:

- Vector2D alias implementation
MathUtil.h forward declares Point2D, then aliases it:

struct Point2D;
using Vector2D = Point2D;

This replaces any uses of Point2D throughout the engine and galaga implementations 
that were better suited to be named as Vector2Ds, such as "offset"s/deltas,
slopes, and directions. Point2D's dot and cross products also take Vector2D parameters.

AI Usage Documentation:

1. ProcessCollisions structure
Prompt: "Walk me through the pseudocode for ProcessCollisions"
        "Can you show me what to put inside the overlap width/height check?"
Reflection: Useful for the order of operations rather than the math. It also caught that
our bounds were still in world coordinates while the shapes were local, which was
inconsistent so we changed it.

2. Contact point when objects have several collision shapes
Prompt: "Now that objects have several collision shapes, the engine stops at the first
        shape pair that reports a hit. How should it pick the contact point instead?"
Reflection: Pointed out that our line functions already returned the earliest point, so
the problem was in the loop above them, which stopped at the first pair of shapes that
collided instead of comparing all of them. We removed the early exit and kept the point
closest to the start of the line.
